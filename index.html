Late deliveries are a promise problem, not a carrier problem.

22% of 16,261 shipped orders arrived after the required date. Promising delivery within a week covers 23% of orders and causes 64% of the late ones. Spread between the best and worst carrier is 0.3 points; for promised lead time it is 62.

Data: the enlarged community version of Microsoft’s Northwind sample database, a fictional trading company. It is a teaching dataset, so read this as a worked method, not a finding about a real business. The what-if ignores commercial effects: customers may not accept longer promises. The link between lead time and lateness is partly mechanical, since an order is late when shipping takes longer than promised, so the useful question is how often promises are shorter than normal shipping time.
